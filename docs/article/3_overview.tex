\section{Overview}

\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/sart_method_figure.png}
    \caption{
    Overview of SART. (A) Per-sample gradient alignment with the validation gradient and answer-token concentration form two complementary geometric signals.
    (B) \textsc{ShortcutScore} combines both signals into a single rank that flags shortcut-prone samples.
    (D) Conflict-only gradient surgery removes only the component of a flagged sample's gradient that genuinely opposes $\mathbf{g}_{\mathcal{V}}$, preserving validation-aligned directions already present in $\mathbf{g}_s$.
    (E) Updates prioritize reasoning signals over shortcut patterns.
    }
    \label{fig:overview}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:overview} shows the framework of SART including two steps: 1) gradient-structure shortcut detection; 2) conflict-only gradient surgery. In Step~1, each training sample is scored from its gradient geometry alone: a per-sample gradient $\mathbf{g}_s$ and a small validation gradient $\mathbf{g}_{\mathcal{V}}$ together yield two signals---\emph{non-transfer alignment} (low cosine with $\mathbf{g}_{\mathcal{V}}$, signaling updates that reduce loss without improving held-out reasoning) and \emph{answer-gradient concentration} (gradient energy dominated by final-answer tokens rather than intermediate reasoning tokens). The two signals are linearly combined into a unified \textsc{ShortcutScore} that flags shortcut-prone samples without needing shortcut labels or environment splits. In Step~2, every flagged sample undergoes a PCGrad-style~\cite{yu2020gradient} conflict-only projection that removes the component of $\mathbf{g}_s$ which genuinely opposes $\mathbf{g}_{\mathcal{V}}$, while leaving positively-aligned directions inside the same sample untouched; samples not flagged retain their original gradient. The conflict-only gate is the load-bearing design choice: it converts shortcut samples from training liabilities into still-usable supervision, instead of discarding them as data filtering does or shrinking them uniformly as scalar reweighting does. Section~\ref{sec:method} formalizes each step.
