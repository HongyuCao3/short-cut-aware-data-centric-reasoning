\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied shortcut-aware reasoning training, where shortcut samples reduce training loss yet collapse generalization the moment numbers, constraints, or phrasing shift. The takeaway is that \textbf{shortcut suppression factors into two orthogonal operations---ranking samples by gradient geometry and projecting away only the component that opposes the validation gradient---so magnitude-only (scalar reweighting) and identity-only (data filtering) corrections leave the second operation undone}. Our framework \textbf{SART} instantiates this view as gradient-structure detection followed by conflict-only directional surgery, requiring no shortcut-group labels and no multi-environment annotation. Across three benchmarks and a controlled-injection pilot at 8B scale, SART is the strongest method on robustness and the only correction primitive whose perturbed-accuracy survives shortcut-flipping on the confounder-laden causal-reasoning split. Three limitations remain: dependence on a clean validation set, incompatibility of answer-token suppression with autoregressive generation at scale, and gradient averaging masking the directional gate at batch granularity. We plan to construct validation probes counterfactually, restrict suppression to numeric answer tokens, and gate per-sample with sketched gradients.
