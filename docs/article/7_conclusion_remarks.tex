\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The central takeaway is not a particular algorithm but a shift in perspective: shortcut mitigation is about which directions to trust during training, not which samples to keep. We instantiate this view in SART, which pairs a \textsc{ShortcutScore} detector with a per-sample, conflict-only projection, and prove the principle under controlled shortcut injection; detection is shown to transfer to pretrained LLMs, while full LLM correction remains a constrained scaling problem under autoregressive generation. Limitations, risks, and future-work directions appear in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_limitations}.
