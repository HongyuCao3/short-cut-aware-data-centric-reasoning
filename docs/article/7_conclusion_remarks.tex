\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
LLMs often rely on reasoning shortcuts rather than genuine logical inference, a failure that stems from \emph{training signal misalignment}: shortcut-encouraging samples efficiently reduce loss but harm generalization. Existing approaches either leave training dynamics unchanged or cannot detect shortcut-promoting gradients. We observe that shortcut samples carry two distinct gradient signatures---low alignment with validation-improving gradients and high answer-token concentration---and build SART on this insight: a \textsc{ShortcutScore} quantifies shortcut propensity, an exponential reweighting reduces its magnitude, and a PCGrad-style conflict-only projection corrects its direction in a single step. On three synthetic benchmarks SART beats the strongest of eleven baselines by +16.5\,pp accuracy and +40.2\,pp robustness, with both components essential (super-additive by +26.7\,pp rob); the detection geometry further transfers cleanly to Qwen3-8B/GSM8K.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations and Risks.}
SART rests on three assumptions. \emph{First}, $\mathbf{g}_{\mathcal{V}}$ is treated as ground truth; if $\mathcal{V}$ is itself contaminated by the same shortcut, conflict-only surgery will \emph{reinforce} rather than suppress it---practitioners should construct $\mathcal{V}$ via human review or counterfactual probes. \emph{Second}, answer-gradient suppression can harm learning when the answer token legitimately carries the dominant supervisory signal (short outputs, classification-style tasks, noisy reasoning chains); the no-CoT fallback (Section~\ref{sec:method}) keeps the suppression term inert via $R(s){=}\tau_R$, but operators should monitor token-level loss. \emph{Third}, flagging a sample as ``shortcut-prone'' is a judgment against a fixed validation objective; under multi-mode supervision (alternative algorithms, multiple valid chains of thought), a mis-specified $\mathcal{V}$ risks collapsing the model onto a single mode and erasing legitimate diversity. SART works best as a targeted intervention on distributions with a canonical reasoning path; open-ended generation needs a diverse $\mathcal{V}$.

\noindent\textbf{Future Work.}
The LLM pilot (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_llm_transfer}) motivates two targeted fixes---restricting $\mathbf{g}_s^{\mathrm{ans}}$ to numeric answer tokens so $\rho{>}0$ no longer destroys generation, and per-sample conflict gating so clean gradients cannot out-vote shortcut gradients. Beyond this, we plan sensitivity analysis on $|\mathcal{V}|$ and its shortcut-consistent fraction, a theoretical analysis of when conflict-only projection preserves stability, and adaptive $(\gamma, \rho)$ scheduling per training phase.
