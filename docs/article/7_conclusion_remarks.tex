\section{Conclusion}
Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have showcased impressive reasoning capabilities, yet these models often rely on reasoning shortcuts rather than genuine logical inference. This reliance, a critical issue within the field, stems from training signal misalignment where shortcuts reduce training loss but hinder generalizable reasoning. Existing approaches fail to adequately address this by either not modifying training dynamics (e.g., Self-Consistency Decoding) or being unable to detect shortcut-promoting gradients (e.g., Data Filtering). This leads to models that appear to reason correctly but fail when distributions shift, resulting in degraded reliability in mission-critical systems and significant financial or safety risks.
%
This study addresses these limitations by developing novel techniques: the ShortcutScore for reweighting training samples and gradient surgery to modify gradient directions. Our technical contributions introduce ShortcutScore-based reweighting and gradient modification strategies that directly improve reasoning robustness and model reliability. We observe that shortcut reasoning samples produce distinct gradient signatures—low alignment with validation gradients and high concentration on answer tokens. This structural insight allows us to quantify shortcut propensity and intervene during training.
%
The experimental results demonstrate that our approach significantly improves reasoning robustness across diverse tasks, substantiating the effectiveness of our methods. Theoretically, this work advances the understanding of gradient alignment and reasoning signal correction, offering new insights into LLM training dynamics. Practically, the implications are profound for applications in domains such as finance and scientific discovery, where enhanced model robustness can lead to more reliable decision-making.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations and Risks.}
SART rests on three assumptions whose failure modes deserve explicit acknowledgment. \emph{First}, the validation gradient $\mathbf{g}_{\mathcal{V}}$ is treated as the ground-truth direction of ``genuine reasoning''; if $\mathcal{V}$ itself contains systematic shortcut contamination---for instance, if the same spurious feature dominates both train and val---then conflict-only surgery will incorrectly project away legitimate reasoning updates and will \emph{reinforce} the shortcut rather than suppress it. Practitioners should construct $\mathcal{V}$ through human review or counterfactual probes, and Section~\ref{sec:exp_setup} prescribes a sensitivity protocol. \emph{Second}, answer-gradient suppression can harm learning when the answer token legitimately carries the dominant supervisory signal (e.g., very short outputs, classification-style tasks, or stages where the reasoning chain is noisy or incomplete); the no-CoT fallback (Section~\ref{sec:method}) defaults to $R(s){=}\tau_R$ to keep the suppression term inert in that regime, but operators should monitor token-level loss to confirm the suppression is not regressing answer accuracy. \emph{Third}, identifying a sample as ``shortcut-prone'' is a judgment against a fixed validation objective; when reasonable humans would accept multiple solution modes (e.g., alternative algorithms in math, multiple valid chains of thought), a mis-specified validation set risks collapsing the model onto the $\mathcal{V}$-preferred mode and erasing legitimate diversity. SART therefore works best as a targeted intervention on distributions with a single canonical reasoning path, and should be paired with a diverse validation set when deployed on open-ended generation tasks.

\noindent\textbf{Future Work.}
Our immediate next steps are to scale SART to real-world reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, SVAMP, StrategyQA) on pretrained LLM backbones via LoRA adapters, to directly compare against meta-reweighting baselines (Ren et al. 2018) and PCGrad-on-validation variants, and to quantify the sensitivity of SART to $|\mathcal{V}|$ and the fraction of shortcut-consistent examples in $\mathcal{V}$. Beyond these, a theoretical analysis of when conflict-only projection preserves training stability, and integration with adaptive schedule methods that tune $\gamma$ and $\rho$ per training phase, would further sharpen the framework.
