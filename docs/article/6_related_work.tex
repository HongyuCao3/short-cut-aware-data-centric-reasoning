\section{Related Work}

Prior literature partially addresses shortcut-aware reasoning training across four categories. \textbf{LLM training and inference-time methods.} Prevailing paradigms---supervised learning, RLHF~\cite{ouyang2022training}, and hybrids surveyed in~\cite{sun2023survey}---optimize answer correctness, structurally favoring shortcuts that reduce training loss via surface pattern matching and answer memorization. Chain-of-Thought prompting~\cite{wei2022chain,kojima2022large} and Self-Consistency decoding~\cite{wang2022self} improve trace generation and output consistency at inference time but leave training dynamics---where shortcut biases are encoded---unchanged. These methods reshape the optimizer's view of training loss or post-process generations at inference, but never look inside the per-sample update; shortcut samples and legitimately-hard samples remain scalar-loss-indistinguishable to them. \textbf{Shortcut learning and annotation-driven invariance.} Models exploit surface statistics rather than causal structure in vision~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut}, rely on syntactic heuristics in NLI~\cite{mccoy2019right}, and achieve high accuracy from hypothesis-only cues~\cite{niven2019probing}. Mitigation either requires explicit shortcut labels (Group DRO~\cite{sagawa2020distributionallyrobustneuralnetworks}), environment partitions (IRM~\cite{arjovsky2020invariantriskminimization}, V-REx~\cite{krueger2021outofdistributiongeneralizationriskextrapolation}, Fishr~\cite{rame2022fishrinvariantgradientvariances}), or operates on scalar loss signals (JTT~\cite{liu2021justtraintwiceimproving}, LfF~\cite{nam2020learningfailuretrainingdebiased}). Real reasoning corpora rarely come with shortcut-group labels or pre-defined environment partitions, and scalar-loss methods cannot tell shortcut samples apart from legitimately difficult ones. \textbf{Validation-driven sample correction.} The closest prior work uses per-sample gradients with a validation signal: meta-reweighting~\cite{ren2018learning} learns per-example scalar weights via second-order gradients, while influence-function filters~\cite{koh2020understandingblackboxpredictionsinfluence} drop samples whose removal most improves held-out performance. Both treat each sample as atomic and act on magnitude or sample identity rather than direction; influence filtering destroys the validation-aligned residual along with the shortcut, and scalar reweighting leaves the harmful direction in the update. \textbf{Gradient surgery and projection.} PCGrad~\cite{yu2020gradient} introduced unconditional task-gradient projection for multi-task learning, and the broader gradient-projection literature~\cite{rosen1960gradient,schulman2015trust,kirkpatrick2017overcoming} formulates parameter updates under directional constraints. These primitives are formulated for unconditional task-gradient conflict or for stability under trust regions, not for per-sample conditional projection on a single-task validation gradient. Our framework therefore inherits the conflict-only projection idea from PCGrad but reframes it as the correction half of a per-sample, label-free shortcut-aware reasoning training pipeline.
