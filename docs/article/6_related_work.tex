\section{Related Work}

\noindent\textbf{LLM training and reasoning.}
Prevailing paradigms---supervised learning, RLHF~\cite{ouyang2022training}, and hybrids surveyed in~\cite{sun2023survey}---optimize answer correctness, structurally favoring shortcuts that reduce training loss via surface pattern matching and answer memorization. Chain-of-Thought prompting~\cite{wei2022chain,kojima2022large} and Self-Consistency Decoding~\cite{wang2022self} improve trace generation and output consistency at inference time but leave training dynamics---where shortcut biases are encoded---unchanged. SART instead targets the training signal at its source.

\noindent\textbf{Shortcut learning and spurious correlations.}
Models exploit surface statistics rather than causal structure in vision~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut}, rely on syntactic heuristics in NLI~\cite{mccoy2019right}, and achieve high accuracy from hypothesis-only cues~\cite{niven2019probing}. Mitigation either requires explicit shortcut labels (Group DRO~\cite{sagawa2020distributionallyrobustneuralnetworks}), environment partitions (IRM~\cite{arjovsky2020invariantriskminimization}, V-REx~\cite{krueger2021outofdistributiongeneralizationriskextrapolation}, Fishr~\cite{rame2022fishrinvariantgradientvariances}), or operates on scalar loss signals (JTT~\cite{liu2021justtraintwiceimproving}, LfF~\cite{nam2020learningfailuretrainingdebiased}) that cannot distinguish shortcut-driven updates from legitimately hard ones. SART instead reads the \emph{gradient geometry} of each sample, which remains informative without group/environment supervision.

\noindent\textbf{Validation-driven reweighting and gradient surgery.}
The closest prior approaches to SART combine per-sample gradients with a validation signal. Meta-reweighting~\cite{ren2018learning} uses second-order gradients to learn per-example weights that minimize validation loss; influence-function filters~\cite{koh2020understandingblackboxpredictionsinfluence} drop samples whose removal most improves held-out performance. Both change \emph{how much} a sample contributes; neither corrects the \emph{direction} of its gradient. Gradient surgery, introduced as PCGrad~\cite{yu2020gradient} for multi-task learning, removes task gradients that conflict at the vector level. SART unifies these threads: a \textsc{ShortcutScore} built from (i) per-sample alignment with $\mathbf{g}_{\mathcal{V}}$ and (ii) answer-gradient concentration flags shortcut-prone examples, a soft reweighting reduces their magnitude, and a PCGrad-style conflict-only projection removes directional components that genuinely oppose validation improvement---targeting both magnitude and direction within a single step, without shortcut annotations or an auxiliary meta-network.
