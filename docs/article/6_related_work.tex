\section{Related Work}

Prior literature partially addresses shortcut-aware training across three strands.

\noindent\textbf{LLM training and inference-time methods.}
Prevailing paradigms---supervised learning, RLHF~\cite{ouyang2022training}, and hybrids surveyed in~\cite{sun2023survey}---optimize answer correctness, structurally favoring shortcuts that reduce training loss via surface pattern matching and answer memorization. Chain-of-Thought prompting~\cite{wei2022chain,kojima2022large} and Self-Consistency Decoding~\cite{wang2022self} improve trace generation and output consistency at inference time but leave training dynamics---where shortcut biases are encoded---unchanged. However, these methods leave training-time parameters intact and cannot distinguish shortcut-driven updates from legitimately hard ones using scalar loss signals alone.

\noindent\textbf{Shortcut learning and annotation-driven invariance.}
Models exploit surface statistics rather than causal structure in vision~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut}, rely on syntactic heuristics in NLI~\cite{mccoy2019right}, and achieve high accuracy from hypothesis-only cues~\cite{niven2019probing}. Mitigation either requires explicit shortcut labels (Group DRO~\cite{sagawa2020distributionallyrobustneuralnetworks}), environment partitions (IRM~\cite{arjovsky2020invariantriskminimization}, V-REx~\cite{krueger2021outofdistributiongeneralizationriskextrapolation}, Fishr~\cite{rame2022fishrinvariantgradientvariances}), or operates on scalar loss signals (JTT~\cite{liu2021justtraintwiceimproving}, LfF~\cite{nam2020learningfailuretrainingdebiased}). However, real reasoning corpora rarely come with shortcut-group labels or pre-defined environment partitions, and scalar-loss methods cannot tell shortcut samples apart from legitimately difficult ones.

\noindent\textbf{Validation-driven correction and gradient surgery.}
The closest prior approaches combine per-sample gradients with a validation signal. Meta-reweighting~\cite{ren2018learning} uses second-order gradients to learn per-example scalar weights that minimize validation loss; influence-function filters~\cite{koh2020understandingblackboxpredictionsinfluence} drop samples whose removal most improves held-out performance. Gradient surgery, introduced as PCGrad~\cite{yu2020gradient} for multi-task learning, removes task gradients that conflict at the vector level. However, scalar reweighting changes only the magnitude of a sample's contribution and leaves a harmful direction intact; influence-based filtering removes the entire sample including its validation-aligned residual; PCGrad is formulated for unconditional task-gradient conflict, not for per-sample conditional projection on a single-task validation gradient. None of these primitives, on its own, both detects shortcut-prone samples without group labels and corrects only the harmful directional component within each such sample.
