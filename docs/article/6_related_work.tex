\section{Related Work}

\noindent\textbf{Language Model Training.}
Prevailing training paradigms---supervised learning, reinforcement learning from human feedback~\cite{ouyang2022training}, and hybrid approaches surveyed in~\cite{sun2023survey}---optimize primarily for answer correctness, which structurally favors shortcuts such as surface pattern matching and answer memorization that efficiently reduce training loss. These shortcuts yield brittle models that fail under distribution shift, relying on spurious correlations rather than causal mechanisms. Our approach targets this failure at its source by modifying training dynamics rather than the objective or architecture.

\noindent\textbf{Reasoning in AI.}
Chain-of-Thought prompting~\cite{wei2022chain,kojima2022large} and Self-Consistency Decoding~\cite{wang2022self} improve reasoning trace generation and output consistency at inference time, but leave training dynamics---where shortcut biases are encoded---unchanged. CoT can produce plausible reasoning steps that still exploit shortcuts, and Self-Consistency detects inconsistencies without correcting the learned parameters that cause them. Effective shortcut suppression requires intervening during training rather than at inference.

\noindent\textbf{Shortcut Learning and Spurious Correlations.}
The shortcut-learning phenomenon has been extensively characterized in vision and NLP: models exploit surface statistics rather than causal structure~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut}, rely on syntactic heuristics in NLI~\cite{mccoy2019right}, and achieve high accuracy from hypothesis-only cues in argument comprehension~\cite{niven2019probing}. Mitigation strategies include group-robust optimization under explicit annotations~\cite{sagawa2020distributionallyrobustneuralnetworks}, invariance penalties across environments~\cite{arjovsky2020invariantriskminimization,krueger2021outofdistributiongeneralizationriskextrapolation,rame2022fishrinvariantgradientvariances}, and two-stage methods that upweight hard samples~\cite{liu2021justtraintwiceimproving,nam2020learningfailuretrainingdebiased}. These methods either require explicit shortcut labels or environment partitions, or operate on scalar loss signals that cannot distinguish shortcut-driven updates from legitimately hard ones. SART instead reads the \emph{gradient geometry} of each sample, which remains informative even without group/environment supervision.

\noindent\textbf{Validation-Driven Reweighting and Gradient Surgery.}
The closest prior approaches to SART combine per-sample gradients with a validation signal. Meta-reweighting methods~\cite{ren2018learning} use second-order gradients to learn per-example weights that minimize validation loss, and influence-function filters~\cite{koh2020understandingblackboxpredictionsinfluence} drop samples whose removal would most improve held-out performance. Both change \emph{how much} a sample contributes; neither corrects the \emph{direction} of its gradient. Gradient surgery, introduced as PCGrad~\cite{yu2020gradient} for multi-task learning, removes task gradients that conflict with one another at the vector level. SART unifies these two threads: a \textsc{ShortcutScore} built from (i) per-sample alignment with the validation gradient and (ii) answer-gradient concentration identifies shortcut-prone examples, a soft reweighting reduces their magnitude, and a PCGrad-style conflict-only projection removes directional components that genuinely oppose validation improvement. The combination targets both the magnitude and the direction of harmful updates within a single optimization step, without requiring shortcut annotations or an auxiliary meta-network.
