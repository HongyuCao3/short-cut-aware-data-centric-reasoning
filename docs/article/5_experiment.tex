\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate SART on three synthetic reasoning benchmarks with controlled shortcut injection, comparing against eleven baselines spanning data-centric, loss-based, distributionally robust, and invariant learning approaches.

%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

\paragraph{Model and Training.} We use a GPT-style transformer (4 layers, $d_{\mathrm{model}}{=}256$, 8 heads, $d_{\mathrm{ff}}{=}1024$, ${\approx}$3.2M params) trained for 40 epochs with AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019decoupled} (lr $1{\times}10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch size 64, cosine annealing). SART hyperparameters ($\alpha{=}\beta{=}1.0$, $\tau_A{=}0.3$, $\tau_R{=}0.5$, $\lambda{=}3.0$, $\gamma{=}1.0$, $\rho{=}0.5$) are selected via an 80-configuration grid search (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_sensitivity}).

\paragraph{Datasets.} We construct three synthetic benchmarks with controlled shortcut injection: 2{,}000 train / 500 val / 1{,}000 test samples each, with training data 70\% shortcut-rule and 30\% true-rule (val/test exclusively follow the true rule; test split evenly into \emph{clean} and \emph{perturbed} by whether the shortcut agrees with the correct answer). \textbf{Math-Arithmetic}: classify $a{+}b{\geq}10$---shortcut $a{\geq}5$, true rule evaluates the sum. \textbf{Financial-Analysis}: regulatory compliance from four features---shortcut revenue${\geq}5$, true rule margin${\geq}5$ \emph{and} debt${<}5$ (multi-feature conjunction). \textbf{Causal-Reasoning}: causal-direction inference with a confounder---shortcut correlation${\geq}5$, true rule $x{\geq}5$ \emph{and} $z{<}3$ (requires confounder adjustment).

\paragraph{Baselines.} We compare against eleven methods spanning four families: \emph{loss / inference-time}---\textbf{SFT}, \textbf{Self-Consistency}~\cite{wang2023selfconsistency} (majority vote over 5 samples, $T{=}0.8$), \textbf{Focal Loss}~\cite{lin2018focallossdenseobject}, and \textbf{JTT}~\cite{liu2021justtraintwiceimproving} (upweight error-prone samples); \emph{data-centric}---\textbf{Data Filtering} (drop samples with post-warmup confidence $>0.90$) and \textbf{Influence Filtering}~\cite{koh2020understandingblackboxpredictionsinfluence}; \emph{invariance / worst-group}---\textbf{Group DRO}$^\dagger$~\cite{sagawa2020distributionallyrobustneuralnetworks}, \textbf{IRM}$^\ddagger$~\cite{arjovsky2020invariantriskminimization}, \textbf{V-REx}$^\ddagger$~\cite{krueger2021outofdistributiongeneralizationriskextrapolation}, \textbf{Fishr}$^\ddagger$~\cite{rame2022fishrinvariantgradientvariances}; and \emph{failure-mining}---\textbf{LfF}~\cite{nam2020learningfailuretrainingdebiased}. $^\dagger$ requires group annotations, $^\ddagger$ requires multiple environments.

\paragraph{Metrics.} All metrics are computed on held-out samples drawn from the true-rule distribution. \textbf{Clean Accuracy} ($\uparrow$) is token-level top-1 accuracy on the clean split (shortcut coincident with the correct label). \textbf{Robustness} ($\uparrow$) is absolute accuracy on the perturbed split, where the shortcut feature \emph{contradicts} the correct label---a model that has truly learned the rule should be unaffected, a shortcut-reliant model flips. \textbf{Reasoning Consistency} ($\uparrow$) is the fraction of test examples whose greedily generated intermediate tokens exactly match the gold chain up to the separator, capturing whether correct answers arrive via the intended path (protocol in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_algorithm}). \textbf{Shortcut Detection F1} ($\uparrow$) is the best binary F1 over a 50-threshold sweep of each method's per-sample score (\textsc{ShortcutScore} for SART, confidence for Data Filtering; methods without a per-sample signal are marked ``--'')---an oracle upper bound on detection quality. The ablation table additionally reports \textbf{Gradient Alignment} $|\cos(\mathbf{g}_s,\mathbf{g}_{\mathcal{V}})|$ over a held-out subset of up to 100 samples; values near $0$ indicate updates neither systematically aligned with nor opposed to the validation-improving direction.

Both components individually beat SFT---\emph{Reweighting Only} adds +6.4\,pp acc / +20.7\,pp rob (and reaches the highest standalone detection F1 of 0.85), and \emph{Gradient Surgery Only} adds +4.7\,pp acc / +21.1\,pp rob---but their combination is strongly super-additive. Robustness gains sum to $20.7{+}21.1{=}41.8$\,pp individually yet reach \textbf{+68.5\,pp} combined, a \emph{+26.7\,pp synergy bonus} (64\% above the sum of parts); accuracy shows a similar +12.6\,pp synergy. The underlying driver is that the two operate on complementary axes---reweighting adjusts \emph{how much} each sample contributes, surgery adjusts \emph{in which direction}; reweighted gradients are more amenable to precise surgical correction, and surgery amplifies reweighting by eliminating residual shortcut directions. The full method drives $|\cos(\mathbf{g}_s,\mathbf{g}_{\mathcal{V}})|$ to $0.03$, a $2.7{\times}$ improvement over SFT, and removing either component incurs nearly symmetric collapses ($-$19.0 / $-$47.4\,pp without Reweighting; $-$17.3 / $-$47.7\,pp without Surgery), confirming both are essential.

\subsection{ShortcutScore Validation and Sensitivity}
\label{sec:exp_detection}

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{results/figure3.png}
    \caption{Empirical validation of \textsc{ShortcutScore}. \textbf{(a)}~Score vs.\ true shortcut rate (Pearson $r=0.67$), showing monotonic correlation. \textbf{(b)}~Gradient alignment distribution: shortcut samples (red) skew negative; non-shortcut samples (green) cluster near zero. \textbf{(c)}~Exponential reweighting curve with $\lambda=3.0$.}
    \label{fig:analysis}
\end{figure}

\textsc{ShortcutScore} reaches an average detection F1 of 0.64---close to Data Filtering's 0.69---yet delivers a +17.8\,pp accuracy advantage over Data Filtering, because SART does not \emph{discard} flagged samples but down-weights them and projects their gradient, preserving any validation-aligned signal the sample still carries. Figure~\ref{fig:analysis}(a) confirms a monotonic $S(s)$--shortcut-rate correlation (Pearson $r{=}0.67$, $p{<}0.001$); panel~(b) shows shortcut gradients skewing toward negative alignment with $\mathbf{g}_{\mathcal{V}}$ while clean gradients cluster near zero. A longer analysis of why detection F1 decouples from downstream gains---why a \emph{lower-F1} action set can outperform a higher-F1 one when the action is soft-reweight + surgery rather than hard drop---appears in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_f1_decoupling}.

\textbf{Hyperparameter sensitivity.} Under the conflict-only rule, the projection strength $\gamma$ should scale with the compositional complexity of the shortcut: $\gamma{=}0.8$ is best for single-feature Math (full projection over-erases useful residual signal), while $\gamma{=}1.0$ is best for multi-feature Financial and Causal, where the conflicting subspace must be fully removed. The answer-suppression coefficient $\rho$ is inactive unless $R(s){>}\tau_R$; it matters only on Causal, where $\rho{=}0{\sim}0.3$ both hit the top row and $\rho{=}0.5$ collapses robustness. The recommended default $(\gamma{=}1.0, \rho{=}0.3)$ improves over the prior default $(\gamma{=}0.8, \rho{=}0.7)$ by $+6.5$\,pp accuracy and $+10.7$\,pp robustness averaged across benchmarks; per-task tuning adds another $+8.2$\,pp robustness. Full sensitivity table and per-dataset breakdown are in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_sensitivity}.

\subsection{Real-World LLM Transfer and Computational Cost}
\label{sec:exp_llm_transfer}

\textbf{GSM8K pilot.} We test whether SART transfers beyond synthetic benchmarks via a controlled-injection pilot on GSM8K with Qwen3-8B + LoRA rank-16 adapters on $q/k/v/o$-proj (70\% sum-of-numbers shortcut relabeling, tested on original-answer splits). Two findings emerge, with full tables in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_llm_transfer}. \emph{Detection transfers cleanly}: \textsc{ShortcutScore} separates injected samples (mean $S{=}0.46$) from clean samples (mean $S{=}0.007$) by more than an order of magnitude, matching the synthetic separation. \emph{Correction does not move accuracy} across the full $(\gamma, \rho, \lambda)$ cross-section tested under a 3-epoch LoRA budget: answer-token suppression at $\rho{>}0$ destroys autoregressive generation (PPL rises five orders of magnitude), and batch-level conflict gating averages clean and shortcut gradients so the gate rarely fires on mixed batches. Promoting gating to per-sample fixes the batch-averaging artifact but still tracks SFT accuracy, and heavy reweighting ($\lambda{=}5$) closes no further gap. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_llm_transfer} records the result as a cleanly negative correction finding at 8B scale and enumerates three concrete follow-ups (numeric-only $\mathbf{g}_s^{\mathrm{ans}}$, hard-cut detection, larger trainable/epoch budget).

\textbf{Computational cost.} SART completes in $\sim$60\,s per synthetic dataset on Apple M-series hardware and under 3\,min per dataset on NVIDIA H100 NVL GPUs, at $\sim$2.5$\times$ the cost of SFT. The overhead comes from per-sample gradient scoring and the surgery step; refreshing $\mathbf{g}_{\mathcal{V}}$ every $k{=}5$ steps (once per epoch for the LLM pilot) keeps the cost amortized.
