\begin{abstract}
Large language models exhibit strong reasoning capabilities, yet often rely on shortcuts---surface pattern matching, memorization, and keyword correlations---rather than genuine logical inference. This stems from \emph{training signal misalignment}: shortcut-encouraging examples reduce training loss but impair generalization when distributions shift or problem phrasing varies. Existing approaches---Chain-of-Thought training, Self-Consistency decoding, data filtering, and RLHF---fail to jointly address shortcut detection from gradient behavior and dynamic suppression during training. We identify a new opportunity: shortcut reasoning samples produce distinct gradient signatures, exhibiting low alignment with validation-improving gradients and high concentration on answer tokens, which enables detection and mitigation through gradient structure analysis rather than data labels alone. We formulate \textbf{shortcut-aware reasoning training} and instantiate it via \textbf{ShortcutScore}-based sample reweighting paired with conflict-only \textbf{gradient surgery}. Experiments across three synthetic reasoning benchmarks with controlled shortcut injection demonstrate significant improvements in robustness and reasoning reliability---a +16.5\,pp accuracy and +40.2\,pp robustness gain over the strongest of eleven baselines---and a pilot LLM transfer on Qwen3-8B/GSM8K confirms the detection geometry transfers to 8B scale. \end{abstract}
