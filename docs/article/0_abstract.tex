\begin{abstract}
Reasoning-capable language models aim to solve problems through genuine logical inference rather than through surface pattern matching. However, training samples that hit the right answer via pattern matching, memorization, or keyword correlations efficiently reduce loss while harming generalization, and because they are correctly labeled they evade data-quality, label-noise, and scalar-loss filters. Existing methods, such as Chain-of-Thought training, Self-Consistency decoding, data filtering, and RLHF, suffer from leaving training dynamics unchanged or requiring shortcut-group annotations practitioners rarely possess. To fundamentally fill this gap, we reformulate shortcut suppression as gradient-geometry analysis and develop a \textbf{S}hortcut-\textbf{A}ware \textbf{R}easoning \textbf{T}raining framework, namely \textbf{SART}. This framework includes two steps: 1) gradient-structure shortcut detection, aiming to flag samples whose gradients oppose a small validation gradient and concentrate on answer tokens; 2) conflict-only gradient surgery, intending to project out the conflicting component while preserving each flagged sample's useful direction. Finally, extensive experiments and case studies demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed method: SART leads robustness across three controlled-shortcut reasoning benchmarks, is the only method whose perturbed accuracy survives shortcut-flipping on a confounder-laden causal split, and its detection geometry transfers cleanly to an 8B-class language model. Code: \url{https://github.com/fuyanjie/short-cut-aware-data-centric-reasoning}.
\end{abstract}
