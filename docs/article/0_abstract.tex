\begin{abstract}
Large language models exhibit strong reasoning capabilities, yet often rely on shortcuts---surface pattern matching, memorization, and keyword correlations---rather than genuine logical inference. This stems from \emph{training signal misalignment}: shortcut-encouraging samples reduce training loss but impair generalization when distributions shift or problem phrasing varies. However, distinguishing such samples from legitimate ones is hard precisely because they are usually correctly labeled and efficient at lowering loss, rendering them invisible to data-quality, label-noise, and scalar-loss signals. Existing methods, such as Chain-of-Thought training, Self-Consistency decoding, data filtering, and RLHF, suffer from leaving training dynamics unchanged or requiring shortcut-group / multi-environment annotation that practitioners rarely possess. To fundamentally fill this gap, we reformulate shortcut suppression as gradient-geometry analysis and develop a \textbf{S}hortcut-\textbf{A}ware \textbf{R}easoning \textbf{T}raining framework, namely \textbf{SART}. This framework includes two steps: 1) gradient-structure shortcut detection, aiming to flag samples whose per-sample gradients are misaligned with a small validation gradient and concentrated on answer tokens; 2) conflict-only gradient surgery, intending to project out only the components that genuinely oppose the validation gradient while preserving the sample's still-useful directions. Finally, extensive experiments and case studies are performed to demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed method---SART is the strongest framework on average across three controlled-shortcut reasoning benchmarks (80.7\% accuracy / 54.8\% robustness, $+12.0$\,pp / $+5.5$\,pp over the strongest baseline) and the only method that exceeds 0.79 perturbed-accuracy on a confounder-laden causal-reasoning split, and the detection geometry transfers cleanly to an 8B-class language model. Code: \url{https://github.com/fuyanjie/short-cut-aware-data-centric-reasoning}.
\end{abstract}
